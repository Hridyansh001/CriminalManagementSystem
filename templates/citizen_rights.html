Citizen Rights & Criminal Justice Acts

Essential statutory rights under the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), Indian Penal Code (IPC), and Supreme Court directives.

1. Mandatory FIR Registration (Section 154 CrPC)

Under Section 154 of the Code of Criminal Procedure and the landmark Supreme Court ruling in Lalita Kumari vs. Govt. of UP (2014), it is mandatory for police officers to register an FIR if the information discloses the commission of a cognizable offense. Refusal to register an FIR can result in penal action against the officer under Section 166A IPC.

2. Free Copy of FIR (Section 154(2) CrPC)

Every citizen who files an FIR is legally entitled to receive a certified printed copy of the registered First Information Report completely free of cost immediately upon filing.

3. Zero FIR Legal Provision

A Zero FIR can be lodged at any police station regardless of where the incident occurred or jurisdiction limits. The receiving police station must record the complaint, initiate immediate inquiry/medical relief, assign a Zero FIR number, and transfer the docket to the jurisdictional police station.

4. Special Safeguards for Women Complainants

In offenses relating to harassment, assault, or stalking, the statement must be recorded by a woman police officer or woman magistrate. Women cannot be arrested after sunset and before sunrise except in exceptional situations with the prior written permission of a Judicial Magistrate.

5. Rights of Arrested Persons (Section 50 & 57 CrPC, D.K. Basu Guidelines)

Any arrested person must be informed immediately of the exact grounds of arrest and the right to bail. An arrest memo must be prepared and a relative or friend must be informed. The arrested person must be produced before the nearest Judicial Magistrate within 24 hours of arrest.

6. Remedy in Case Police Refuse to Register FIR (Section 154(3) & 156(3) CrPC)

If an officer refuses to register an FIR, the complainant can send the substance of the information in writing by registered post to the Superintendent of Police (SP / DCP) under Section 154(3) CrPC, or file an application before the Judicial Magistrate under Section 156(3) CrPC to order a police investigation.
